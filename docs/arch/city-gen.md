# City generation: roads, land use, traffic (A3)
Status: chosen 2026-10-12 (mgr-city). Code: crates/city (pure, no ECS). Replaces the interim crates/app/src/layout.rs.

## Survey (what exists, what we take)
- Parish & Mueller 2001 (L-system roads): rules grown against *global goals* (pattern, population) and *local constraints* (water, snapping to nearby junctions, intersection repair). Take: the constraint/snap loop, priority-ordered growth. Reject: the L-system rewriting itself (hard to steer around terrain, no valley following).
- Chen, Esch, Wonka, Mueller, Zhang 2008 (tensor fields): streets are hyperstreamlines of a blended tensor field (grid, radial, boundary-aligned bases). Take: one cheap field (grid near the core, curvilinear noise-rotated in suburbs, shoreline-aligned near water) traced by streamline integration, with snap/T-junction termination. Reject: full separatrix-based seeding and Poisson streamline placement (large, needs a UI to author fields).
- Kelly & McCabe, Citygen 2007: primary roads as least-cost paths over a terrain-cost grid, secondary streets subdividing the enclosed cycles. Take: primary/arterial routing by least-cost path (so arterials follow valleys/flats and coasts, and cross water only where it is narrow, at a bridge price). Reject: the cycle-subdivision step (we trace streets, which yields cul-de-sacs the cycle method cannot).
- Agent-based growth (Lechner et al. 2003/04, Weber et al. 2009, Vanegas et al. 2009): land-use and road agents reacting to access and neighbours. Take: land-use follows access (commercial on arterials and junctions, industry near water and at the edge, housing behind); growth is time-ordered. Reject: per-agent runtime simulation (cost scales with agents, violates D-2).
- Watabou Medieval Fantasy City Generator: gate-to-plaza shortest paths, Voronoi wards. Take: the village seed idea (everything radiates from a core/crossing). Reject: Voronoi block geometry, wrong feel for modern suburbs.

## Choice: terrain-cost arterials + tensor-field local streets + access-gated growth
1. Seed: a crossroads (two arterials crossing at the core), or a river-crossing village when water lies within ~30 units of the core (the first arterial bridges the narrowest water, so the settlement starts at the crossing).
2. Arterials: least-cost path (Dijkstra, 4-unit grid) from the core to gateway targets 55-80 units out. Cost = 1 + slope + coast pull; narrow water (<= MAX_BRIDGE across some axis) is passable at bridge price, wide water is not. A bypass arterial links two neighbouring gateways. Paths are Douglas-Peucker simplified, then cut into edges <= 14 units.
3. Local streets: seeds on every other arterial node, both sides, heading perpendicular to the arterial snapped to the tensor field; traced 5-unit steps; stop at water (no local bridges), snap to nodes/edges within reach (T-junction, edge split), length limit by distance to core. Field: grid aligned with the core axes within ~25 units; away from the core the angle is rotated by smooth noise (curvilinear); near water it blends toward the shoreline tangent. Suburban streets end in cul-de-sacs with probability rising with distance. Depth-2 streets branch off depth-1 streets to knit blocks.
4. Parcels (zones): placed both sides of every edge at 7-unit spacing, clear of other roads and water, thinned with distance. Land use by access: commercial = core, arterial frontage near the core, junctions; industrial = within ~14 units of water (not at the core) or arterial frontage at the town edge; residential = the rest (local streets). Ribbon development falls out of the order below.
5. Build order: every edge gets `order` = graph distance from the core node. The seed (order <= SEED_ORDER) is built at start. Parcels carry `access` = frontage edge; a zone grows only once that edge is built.

## Roads are the graph
The generated graph is the road network. `transit` mirrors built edges as Road edges (zone node -> access node edge once the frontage is built), so station links, growth cost matrix and accessibility all see real roads. Cars route on the same graph in `city::traffic`.

## Road growth (D-7)
`city::roadbuild`: the city builds at a rate = GROWTH_RATE * ROADS_PER_RATE (one tunable). A road is *planned* (ghost) LEAD ticks before being built. Priority: frontier edges next to a fully developed street first. growth::RoadAgency still emits underserved-zone plans (transit share < s_min) which pull the nearest frontier edge ahead of the queue, so poor transit makes the city build more road (car-dependence loop). Both feed one ghost list and one reserve figure.

## Traffic and mode choice
Per assignment (every TRAFFIC_EVERY ticks): trips = gravity over car travel time, split work/shop with time-of-day peaks (day = DAY_TICKS); car time = shortest path on built roads with BPR congestion on smoothed edge flows; transit time = access + wait + rail ride + egress over the player network (`city::transit_times`, computed on the ECS side when transit changes); mode share = logit on (transit - car - CAR_BIAS). Car flow = (1 - share) * demand is assigned to edges (MSA smoothing, deterministic). Outputs per edge: flow, v/c, speed; per zone transit share; city totals -> CarShare, Scorecards.
Render: low zoom = v/c tint on road strokes; high zoom = capped pool of car dots whose count follows q*t (Little's law), bunching toward the downstream end as v/c rises.
Known simplifications (ponytail): single-pass MSA not equilibrium; no turn/junction delay model (queues are a render bunching trick); destination choice on car time; transit has flat wait/no transfer penalty until C1 trains exist.
